eyEar: An AI Navigation Wearable for the Blind

A $50 camera clip for glasses that gives blind users spoken directions for everyday tasks. MIT Solve Top 10 National Finalist and 3rd Place nationally at the Jacobson Institute Innovator Competition.

Overview

For most blind people, everyday tasks like finding a seat, reading a menu, or knowing who just walked in still mean asking someone for help. The tools meant to change that either cost $2,000-$4,500 or only give generic information ("there is a table"), so users end up relying on sighted companions anyway. I built eyEar to let them do those things on their own for under $50 using a XIAO ESP32-S3 Sense inside a 3D-printed clip. Paired with my Flutter app, it captures JPEG images and searches my database of 100+ scenarios to find the most accurate one. Within two seconds, it speaks an action ("empty seat, 2 o'clock, 4 steps"). Ajay, who is blind, co-designed the scenario database, which I call Cortex, with me alongside two other blind testers.
